The lead magnet

Short planner (two pages): When a Staff Complaint Lands: Your First Steps Planner

A short planner for the first days after a staff complaint lands, such as a bullying complaint between people at work. It helps the person who has to act set out what has been raised, see whether the people who'd normally handle it are too close to it, weigh whether it needs a formal investigation, and finish with a short snapshot of the scope. It is for complaints about staffing matters, not resident, client or participant complaints or reportable incidents.

The main points it would cover:

  • Start from the complaint that has landed on their desk, and set out what has been raised
  • Think through what needs care in the first days: support for the people involved, who needs to know, and keeping it confidential new
  • Formal investigation, or can this be handled another way? A few questions to weigh, never a ruling new
  • Whether the people who'd normally handle it are too close to it, or have never run an investigation
  • A scope snapshot: the nature of the complaint, the key issues, the outcome sought, the people, witnesses, documents and the internal support available
  • Where a matter involves complex legal interpretation, significant legal risk or formal legal representation, you recommend bringing in specialist legal advice
  • What happens next with you: an initial discussion, then the Terms of Reference

What's inside, in order:

PartWhat it does
What's been raisedSpace to note the complaint in plain facts: what has been raised, by whom, when and how, kept separate from anyone's opinion of it
What needs care nowA short list to tick through in the first days: support for the people involved, who needs to know, and keeping it confidential while it is looked into
Formal investigation, or another way?A few questions to weigh, with room to note their thinking, and your note that complex legal matters are best taken to specialist legal advice
Who's too close to itBoxes to tick on whether the people who'd normally handle it are too close to it or have never run an investigation, and whether someone from outside is needed
Your scope snapshotIn the same order as your initial discussion: the nature of the complaint, the key issues, the outcome they're seeking, the people to be interviewed, potential witnesses, documents and records, and the internal support available
Your next stepHow an initial discussion with you works, and how the Terms of Reference follow it, so they know what happens if they'd like help

How your lead would use it

The CEO, operations manager or the one person looking after HR fills it in at their desk in the first days after a complaint lands, in one short sitting. They keep it in front of them for their next conversation, with their leadership team or with you.

The free guides from SafeWork SA, Creative Workplaces and others explain good practice for handling complaints, for every kind of workplace, including when to bring in someone from outside, and some sites offer a general tick list. This planner gives more in two ways. It is filled in for the lead's own staff complaint, in the first days, before anything has been decided, with the question of who is too close to it as part of that. And it ends with a scope snapshot in the same order as your initial discussion, so their next conversation, with their leadership team or with you, starts from one page.
