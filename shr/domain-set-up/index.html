The contact email is the one to look at closely. It's the address the register holds for whoever looks after this domain. If it's yours, or one your business keeps, that's a good sign. If it belongs to someone else, that's the part to change.

That's your business's name, which is how it should be. The name is only half of it, though. The sign-in matters just as much: if the login belongs to someone else (a web developer, or someone who has since left), you don't fully own your domain, even with your name on it.

In all my years of doing this, I see this a lot. It's okay, but it's something to change, because like everything in your business, you should own the asset completely.

  • What Cloudflare is. A free, widely used control panel for a domain. It holds the list of records that tell the internet where your website and your email live.

Create Your Cloudflare Account

  1. Press the button. It opens Cloudflare's sign-up page.
  2. Email: type info@simplyhumanresources.com.au.
  3. Password: choose one that's used nowhere else.
  4. If Cloudflare asks you to tick a box to show you're a person, tick it.
  5. Click Create Account.
  6. If Cloudflare offers other ways to sign up, please use the email and password boxes, so the account belongs to the business.
  7. Cloudflare sends you an email. Open it and click the link to confirm your address.
  8. Save the password wherever you keep your passwords.
  9. If Cloudflare asks what you'd like to do first, or offers a tour or a paid plan, you can skip it. The next step starts from the Domains page.

2

Add Your Domain

  1. In Cloudflare, open Domains, then click Onboard a domain.
  2. Type simplyhumanresources.com.au exactly, with no www in front.
  3. Cloudflare asks how you'd like to add your records. Keep the choice where it finds your existing records by itself. That's how everything comes along.
  4. Click Continue.
  5. Choose the Free plan. It's all that's needed. If a paid plan or an extra is offered, you don't need it.
  6. Cloudflare now shows a list of the records it found, under Review DNS records. It looks technical. You don't need to read it or understand it.
  7. Beside some records is a switch with an orange cloud and the word Proxied. Others show a grey cloud and DNS only. Leave every switch exactly as Cloudflare shows it, whether it's on or off. I set these myself during my check.
  8. A column called TTL shows Auto. Leave it. It's only how often a record is refreshed.
  9. Please don't add, edit or delete anything in the list. If a record looks wrong or missing, leave it: I check every one.
  10. Scroll to the bottom of the list and click Continue.
  11. If Cloudflare shows a warning or asks you to confirm something here, please stop and press Ask Adam.
  12. Cloudflare now shows two nameservers and asks you to update them. Please stop here for now. That part is step 4, after my check.

4

Make The One Switch

  1. Only once I've told you the check is finished.
  2. Sign in to GoDaddy, where your domain is registered.
  3. Open simplyhumanresources.com.au and find its nameservers setting. It may be called Nameservers, Name servers or DNS.
  4. If you see a setting called DNSSEC that is switched on, please stop and press Ask Adam.
  5. Remove every nameserver listed there now.
  6. Add these two, exactly as shown:
    austin.ns.cloudflare.com
    noor.ns.cloudflare.com
  7. Save.
